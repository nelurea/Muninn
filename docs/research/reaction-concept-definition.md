# Reaction Concept Definition

## Status

Accepted for further design exploration.

This document summarizes the conceptual investigation performed in Issue #80.

---

## Definition

Reaction represents:

> What changed in me after experiencing a work?

Reaction captures the psychological, cognitive, or behavioral changes that occurred as a result of engaging with a work.

Examples:

* I felt motivated
* I calmed down
* I gained confidence
* I wanted to create
* My perspective changed
* I wanted to learn more
* I resumed practicing

---

## Relationship to Existing Personal Context

Current Personal Context concepts:

* Purpose
* State
* Attraction
* Focus
* Response

Reaction is proposed as an additional dimension.

### Attraction

What drew my attention?

Examples:

* Color palette
* Composition
* Character expression
* Worldbuilding

### Focus

What was I paying attention to?

Examples:

* Background details
* Lighting
* Hand gestures

### Response

How did I perceive or feel about the work?

Examples:

* Beautiful
* Interesting
* Melancholic
* Inspiring

### Reaction

What changed because of the work?

Examples:

* I felt calmer
* I felt encouraged
* I wanted to create
* I wanted to practice
* I gained a new perspective

---

## Conceptual Flow

A tentative relationship between concepts:

Attraction
→ Response
→ Reaction

Example:

Attraction:
Worldbuilding

Response:
Beautiful

Reaction:
I wanted to create

Reaction is not a replacement for Response.

Response describes perception and feeling.

Reaction describes effect and change.

---

## Exclusions

Reaction should not contain:

### Attraction

* What attracted attention
* Aesthetic qualities
* Visual features

### Focus

* Areas of attention
* Objects of analysis

### Purpose

* Reasons for saving

### Response

* Immediate impressions
* Emotional evaluations

### Retrieval Intent

* When to revisit
* How the work should be used later

These concepts remain separate.

---

## Evaluation Outcome

The concept appears viable because:

1. It contains information not represented by existing Personal Context dimensions.
2. It occupies a clear position within the Attraction → Response → Reaction chain.
3. It aligns with Muninn's goal of enriching the relationship between a user and a captured work.
